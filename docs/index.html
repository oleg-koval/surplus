A small local decision

A small decision at launch.

Surplus reads the usage window the provider exposes, checks your reserve and reset time, then decides whether to apply your stronger settings.

  1. 01

    Read what the provider reports

    Usage and policy state stay on your device. Surplus checks reported allowance, reset time and included-usage permission locally at launch and makes no model call to measure them.

  2. 02

    Check the pace and reserve

    Surplus projects how much allowance would remain at reset. Stronger settings need room above your reserve, with at least 25% left in each reported usage window.

  3. 03

    Keep your normal choices

    Missing or stale data, explicit model choices and non-interactive sessions keep the provider's usual settings.

iA 5% reserve and 5% expected-use budget are part of the weekly check. These are routing thresholds, not a cap on a running session. Surplus cannot promise a model-specific quota or prevent paid overage.

npm install --global surplus-cli
surplus install

Questions

What people ask.

Why change the effort or model at all, and is high always better?

Allowance expires at the reset, so unused allowance is wasted. Surplus applies the stronger setting only when the pace projection says allowance would otherwise go unused or the near-reset rule applies. Higher effort spends more of the same allowance. An explicit model or effort flag, or a provider model variable, always passes through unchanged.

What happens during a burst of heavy sessions?

Every interactive launch eligible for automatic routing is checked again. Launches with an explicit model or effort override, and non-interactive launches, pass through unchanged, and a running session is never switched. Premium needs at least 25% of the week left, at least 25% of the five-hour window where reported, and, for pace-based routing, a projection that still leaves at least 15% unused at the reset. The near-reset strategy does not require this projection threshold. While premium is already active, the weekly floor relaxes to 20% and the pace-based projected-unused floor to 10% (the five-hour floor stays 25%), so a burst tends to stay premium until one of them is hit. For Claude that happens only once a refreshed statusline sample reflects the burn, so launches before it still go premium only while an estimated debit of 1% of the week per premium launch (configurable, 0 disables) leaves room. A newer reading, a new reset window, or another account clears the estimate.

Why can a burst overshoot on Claude?

Claude launches read the last sample a running session saved, accepted for up to 120 minutes, so several launches before the next refresh would see the same number. Surplus holds back an estimated 1% of the week per premium Claude launch (premiumLaunchDebitPercent, 0 disables) until the next reading, which is only a guess, not a measurement. Codex is read at each launch. surplus forecast claude 20 keeps premium off unless the reserve plus 20% of the week is left. It does not change the pace projection, and it needs a saved Claude reading, which a running session's statusline writes. With defaults that reserve is 25%, the same as the normal floor, so it only matters once premium is already active. Details in the README ↗

How much headroom do I have for an unexpectedly busy day?

Surplus does not predict spikes. It keeps fixed floors: at least 25% of the week left and, for pace-based routing, a projection at premium burn (1.5x Claude, 1.3x Codex) that leaves at least 15% unused at the reset. The near-reset strategy does not require this projection threshold. Once premium is active, the weekly floor relaxes to 20% and the pace-based projected-unused floor to 10%. Whatever sits above those floors is your cushion. For a known heavy stretch, surplus forecast claude 30 reserves more until the reset. Automatic busy-day headroom is not built yet.

What data does Surplus keep, and can it stop charges?

Surplus sends no analytics or usage events, and keeps its state in local XDG directories. It cannot prevent charges from paid-overage settings already enabled in a provider account, and it never changes billing settings.
